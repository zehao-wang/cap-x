\title{\textbf{What cap-x Lacks: Gaps Found by Dogfooding\\
a Drawer-Opening Skill on LIBERO-PRO}}
\author{cap-x-se / self-evolve dogfooding}

\texttt{capx-se/open\_drawer/})}}

\begin{abstract}
We probe what the cap-x agent framework is missing by \emph{dogfooding}: hand-authoring
a LIBERO drawer-opening skill using only cap-x's legitimate perception + planning
vocabulary (agentview / wrist RGB-D $+$ proprioception in, joint actions out; \emph{no}
privileged simulator state on the solve path), running it live, and recording every place
the stack fell short. The exercise both (a) opens the drawer robustly and safely
---\textbf{a cold-start runner now succeeds 5/5} (\code{qpos} $0\!\to\!-0.160$, object
disturbance 4--25\,mm), up from a 3/5 naive baseline that plowed through the scene
(bowl $\sim$210\,mm)---and (b) surfaces a coherent set of framework gaps. The headline
gap: cap-x has \textbf{no deterministic short-range ``seat'' primitive}; its only motion
engine is a stochastic trajectory optimizer whose final-grasp landing height scatters by
RNG, so precision grasping is non-repeatable. Beyond that we find a self-inconsistent
task/eval contract, a motion stack of half-wired pieces (a collision-aware planner that had
never run from the reduced API), an execution model that fights the fixed eval horizon, no
contact/collision feedback to the skill, and no headless multi-round execution loop. Legend
throughout: \patched{} fixed this session; \flag{} needs a decision; \built{} new tooling
added.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup: the dogfooding contract}
The task is the LIBERO \code{open\_the\_middle\_drawer\_of\_the\_cabinet} scenario
(\code{libero\_goal} task 0; success $=$ drawer \code{qpos} $< -0.14$). The hard rules
(from \code{AGENT.md}) are the point of the exercise:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{No privileged state on the solve path.} Object/joint ground truth a real
        robot cannot read is forbidden; everything is estimated from cameras $+$
        proprioception. Privileged reads are allowed \emph{only} in the runner, for scoring.
  \item \textbf{Success is necessary but not sufficient --- it must be SAFE.} The skill must
        not collide with or disturb other objects; the runner reports task success
        \emph{and} object disturbance. Safety dominates success: fail safely rather than
        succeed unsafely.
  \item \textbf{No overfitting} to a single task/seed; closed-loop on sensing feedback.
        Multi-step interaction (drive a segment, observe, drive the next) and the wrist
        eye-in-hand camera are encouraged.
\end{itemize}
The legitimate vocabulary --- SAM3 text segmentation, depth$\to$3D, Contact-GraspNet,
pyroki/CuRobo IK and motion, the blocking joint controller, gripper control --- is
\emph{sufficient in principle}: from perception alone we detect the three handles, pick
``middle'' from the instruction, estimate the prismatic axis, grasp, and pull the drawer
fully open. The primitives are sound; the gaps are in how they are assembled, observed, and
made repeatable.

\section{The headline gap: no deterministic short-range seat}
\label{sec:seat}
cap-x's only motion engine is the HORL/pyroki \textbf{trajectory optimizer} (trajopt). For
long-range \emph{transit} it is fine. For the final $\sim$5\,cm \emph{seat} --- dropping the
gripper precisely onto a thin handle bar --- it is the wrong tool, and this was the single
root cause of unreliable success.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Non-repeatable landing.} The \emph{same} seat target lands the TCP at
        $z$ scattering \textbf{0.10--0.17 by RNG seed}. The middle handle is a horizontal
        D-bracket bar at $z\!\approx\!0.110$; only a vertically centered grip
        ($z\!\approx\!0.10$) drags the drawer. A landing 1--6\,cm high grips \emph{above}
        the bar and pulls nothing. With a warm planner (RNG advanced) we hit good seats
        $\to$ 5/5 in-harness; with a cold planner (one process per seed) the seat RNG gated
        success.
  \item \textbf{Untrustworthy cost.} The trajopt intermittently returns NaN final cost
        (diverged) and a \emph{phantom} $\sim$$10^{10}$ collision cost for a gripper passing
        7\,cm \emph{above} a flat object whose real disturbance is $\sim$3\,mm. The cost
        cannot be a safety gate.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\patched{} (worked around). We replaced the trajopt seat with a
\textbf{deterministic} \code{solve\_ik} seat: solve IK at the bar pose, then linearly
interpolate joints over the pre$\to$bar corridor (verified clear by the sensing point cloud).
This lands the TCP repeatably deep and centered (measured $z = 0.109/0.110/0.110$ over three
reps vs the trajopt's 0.10--0.17), and on the cold runner it lands on-bar on the \emph{first
try} every seed. Caveat: pyroki IK quality depends on a good warm-start --- called from a
high arm config it returns a $z\!\approx\!0.14$ solution --- so the seat re-descends and
re-solves if it lands off-bar.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{What cap-x needs:} a first-class deterministic short-range seat
primitive (direct IK at the grasp pose $+$ collision-checked linear interp over a clear
corridor). The planner is right for transit and wrong for the precision seat; the framework
should ship both.

\section{The task / eval contract is not self-consistent}
\label{sec:contract}
LIBERO-PRO's \code{\_task} variant perturbs the BDDL. For
\code{open\_the\_middle\_drawer\_of\_the\_cabinet} we found three disagreeing sources of
truth (Table~\ref{tab:contract}). \code{load\_libero\_task} \emph{deliberately} fed the
agent the suite-metadata language (``middle''), but \code{check\_success()} scores the BDDL
goal (``bottom''). \textbf{Following the instruction can never pass}, and the entire
\code{\_task}/\code{\_swap} arm is silently mis-scored. Verified: opening
\code{middle\_level} $\to$ \code{check\_success() = False}; setting
\code{bottom\_level} $= -0.16$ $\to$ \code{True}.

\begin{table}[h]\centering
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
source & value \\
\midrule
filename & \code{open\_the\_middle\_drawer\dots} \\

suite metadata \code{task.language} & \textbf{``open the middle drawer of the cabinet''} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Three disagreeing ``truths'' for one perturbed task.}
\label{tab:contract}
\end{table}

\noindent\patched{} \code{load\_libero\_task} now reads the BDDL \code{(:language \dots)}
(the same source as the scored goal), prefers it, and warns when it diverges from suite
metadata. Non-perturbed suites are unaffected. \flag{} This flips the instruction shown to
the agent across every perturbed task --- it needs confirmation that BDDL-as-truth is the
intended scoring for the PRO suites. \textbf{Framework invariant:} instruction and reward
must come from one source, and the loader should \emph{assert} they agree (or log loudly).

\section{The motion stack is a collection of half-wired pieces}
A capability the agent is \emph{told} it has but that errors on first call is worse than not
having it. The only collision-aware planner (CuRobo) had never run from the reduced API:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \patched{} \textbf{\code{get\_ee\_pose} never existed.}
        \code{update\_curobo\_world\_with\_object} and \code{plan\_with\_grasped\_object}
        call \code{self.get\_ee\_pose()}, but no class defined it (the in-repo example codes
        call it too --- they could never have run). Added
        \code{FrankaLiberoApiReduced.get\_ee\_pose()} reading \code{robot\_cartesian\_pos}
        (real-robot legitimate).
  \item \patched{} \textbf{warp API drift.} Vendored CuRobo
        \code{geom/sdf/world\_mesh.py} calls \code{wp.torch.device\_from\_torch}, removed in
        warp 1.14. Every collision-world build raised \code{AttributeError}. Added a compat
        shim.
  \item \patched{} \textbf{Not exposed.} \code{plan\_grasp\_trajectory} /
        \code{plan\_with\_grasped\_object} / \code{execute\_joint\_trajectory} /
        \code{parse\_grasp\_poses\_for\_curobo} were commented out of \code{functions()}.
        Exposed once the above made them work.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent After the fixes the CuRobo IK path works and is \textbf{accurate ($\sim$5\,mm)},
far better than pyroki for low grasps. Remaining issues:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \flag{} \textbf{Collision-aware reach-into-container fails.}
        \code{plan\_grasp\_trajectory(use\_world\_collision=True)} returns
        \code{GRAPH\_FAIL} / ``Start or End state in collision'' for a handle adjacent to the
        cabinet mesh (home config flagged in-collision; goal grazes the scene mesh). Workable
        only collision-off. Needs better scene-collision ignoring / sphere-buffer tuning.
  \item \flag{} \textbf{\code{solve\_ik} silently substitutes orientation.} It tries the
        requested quaternion, then silently falls back to top-down / 45$^\circ$ / side and
        returns whatever solves --- the caller cannot tell which it got. For a directional
        drawer grasp this breaks Cartesian continuity. Want a \code{strict=True} mode (raise
        instead of re-orienting) or return the achieved orientation.
  \item \flag{} \textbf{pyroki IK is inaccurate at low/awkward poses.} The soft
        least-squares solver left $\sim$5\,cm error at the low handle ($z\!\approx\!0.04$),
        grasping above it; CuRobo IK hit 5\,mm. Route precision moves through CuRobo IK, or
        tighten the pyroki cost/iterations.
  \item \flag{} \textbf{RRT to a near-structure goal fails silently --- and hangs.}
        \code{planner.plan(\dots)} to a pre-grasp near the cabinet intermittently returns
        not-planned with ``RRTConnect: start tree could not be initialized'' (start config
        reads in-collision against the depth cloud). The caller gets a silent no-op and,
        unless it \emph{verifies} the achieved pose, proceeds from the wrong place (stranded
        high $\to$ a too-high seat). Worse: after the pull, with the arm parked at the open
        drawer, RRT reads the \emph{start} as in-collision and \textbf{hangs the whole run}
        though the drawer is already open. We worked around both by retrying/verifying and by
        using trajopt for the retreat. Needs a reliable near-contact RRT or a documented
        ``verify the move landed'' contract.
  \item \flag{} The HTTP CuRobo client (\code{capx/integrations/motion/curobo.py}) is
        \textbf{dead code}; the pyroki \code{/plan} route silently ignores obstacles. One
        motion facade with a tested contract should subsume these, and the dead paths should
        be deleted.
\end{itemize}

\section{Execution model fights the fixed-horizon eval}
\flag{} \code{move\_to\_joints\_blocking} spends up to \code{max\_steps} sim-steps
\emph{per waypoint}. \code{execute\_joint\_trajectory(subsample=1, max\_steps=400)} over a
30-waypoint plan blew the default episode horizon $\to$ robosuite ``executing action in
terminated episode''. Conversely the default \code{max\_steps=120} under-converged (joints
0.39\,rad short, $\sim$5\,cm off) until raised to $\sim$250--400, and it stops short
\emph{silently}. Needs a horizon-aware (time-parameterized, budget-checked) trajectory
executor and an explicit convergence-failure signal the skill can branch on.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Cross-suite evidence.} Running the \emph{same} skill on the
position-perturbed \code{libero\_goal\_swap/task0} (the cabinet is repositioned ---
handle at $x\!\approx\!0.41$ vs $0.70$ in the base suite) is a clean demonstration. The
deterministic seat generalized (on-bar grip 0.164 on the first try), but with the default
30000-step horizon the run died ``executing action in terminated episode'' \emph{mid-pull}:
the further/deeper cabinet pose makes the blocking pull more step-expensive, and the
cumulative per-waypoint cost over the ratcheting pull simply does not fit. Raising the
horizon to 80000 the \emph{identical} skill \textbf{succeeds} --- drawer fully open
(\code{qpos} $-0.159$), object disturbance 21.9\,mm (wine bottle; bowl/cheese/plate
$0.0$). So a \emph{correct, safe} skill fails or passes purely on the execution-step budget
of the position controller --- on a real fixed-horizon eval this is a silent, layout-induced
failure, not a planning or perception error.

\section{No contact / collision feedback to the skill}
\flag{} The bottom drawer fails because the hand-collision box hits the table and the gripper
clips the stacked upper handles and a plate --- but the skill is \textbf{blind} to all of it
(we saw it only via privileged \code{sim.data.contact}). With perception only, the skill
cannot distinguish ``didn't reach'' from ``reached but blocked''. Expose the proprioceptive
collision signals a real robot also has --- commanded-vs-achieved pose error, joint-effort /
contact spikes, gripper-didn't-close-on-anything --- as first-class API outputs, and let the
planner plan against the \emph{observed} scene mesh so it routes around obstacles instead of
\code{GRAPH\_FAIL}-ing because the goal grazes a mesh.

\section{Control mode: position-only, no compliance}
\flag{} The deepest blocker for the (unreachable) bottom drawer is the control mode. cap-x
drives a \textbf{blocking \code{JOINT\_POSITION} controller} against a \textbf{coarse gripper
collision box} (half-extent 0.104). Measured floor: the wrist cannot descend below
$z\!\approx\!0.09$ near the table, but the bottom handle is at $z = 0.042$ --- so the
end-effector physically cannot reach it from the front/side, and top-down is blocked by the
stacked upper drawers. Every primitive (CuRobo, pyroki IK, hook/drag, graspnet) hits this
same wall. Trained LIBERO policies open it because they use \textbf{compliant OSC} that
pushes through soft table/cabinet contacts a position controller treats as hard stops.
Expose an operational-space / impedance mode (or a contact-tolerant guarded-move primitive);
this is likely the single most important missing capability for low/cluttered targets.

\section{Grasp synthesis is too weak for thin/cluttered handles}
\flag{} Contact-GraspNet on a thin handle and a top-down heuristic do not cover a low handle
wedged above a table, below stacked handles, with a plate in front; graspnet returns only
low-confidence / degenerate grasps there. cap-x also has \emph{no} primitive for a
front/horizontal grasp or for estimating an articulation axis --- PCA plane-fit on the
cabinet points gives the wrong axis ($-X$; truth $+Y$) because depth sees front$+$top faces.
We worked around it by estimating the axis from cabinet-centroid$\to$handle and then
\emph{probing} (pull a little, re-observe the handle's 3D displacement). Add a non-prehensile
``hook and drag'' primitive and integrate grasp selection with the collision world so only
\emph{reachable} grasps are proposed. (AnyGrasp $+$ a real collision world is the expected
fix; until then, some initial scenes where graspnet finds no viable grasp are simply skipped.)

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Cross-suite evidence.} On \code{libero\_goal\_task/task0} (with the
\S\ref{sec:contract} patch the instruction is now ``open the bottom drawer'', matching the
scored goal), the skill correctly detects the bottom handle at $z = 0.042$ and
\emph{safe-aborts} --- it cannot reach a clear pre-grasp (the handle sits below the wrist
floor), so it grasps nothing and returns failure rather than forcing it. Correct behavior,
but with a sting: the abort logged ``zero disturbance'' while the plate actually moved
\textbf{36.3\,mm} --- the descend-to-pre fallback trajopt (reporting a $\sim$$2\times10^{9}$
collision cost) grazed the plate \emph{before} giving up. So even the \emph{abort} path is
not collision-verified on execution; the skill's safety claim was trusting the planner's
intent, not its executed motion. This reinforces \S\ref{sec:seat}'s lesson (gate on executed
sensing, not trajopt cost) and \S9's: \emph{every} stage, including bail-outs, needs a
guarded/collision-checked executor.

\section{Robustness: a ``success'' that knocks things over is not a solution}
\flag{} On the \emph{successful} naive middle-drawer run the arm displaced the bowl
$\sim$210--240\,mm, cream cheese $\sim$100\,mm, plate $\sim$40\,mm --- a real-robot failure.
Causes and findings:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Collision-off motion plows through the scene; the front-grasp corridor passes right
        over the bowl in front of the cabinet.
  \item Collision-aware CuRobo works but is finicky: default robot spheres flag the start
        in-collision ($\to$\code{GRAPH\_FAIL}); shrinking spheres plans but then the
        under-sized model grazes obstacles for real. Full-size $+$ larger
        \code{robot\_distance\_threshold} both plans and cuts disturbance to $\sim$29\,mm in
        isolation.
  \item But it is non-deterministic in the full pipeline: the joint-space plan to the IK
        solution (\code{plan\_single\_js}) isn't world-collision-checked the way the pose plan
        is, so the executed path still sometimes grazes the bowl ($\sim$120\,mm). The
        collision guarantee is only as strong as the weakest stage.
  \item A single-view world mesh (marching cubes from one agentview depth) underestimates
        partially-occluded objects, so even a correct planner routes too close.
  \item The pull is not collision-aware/constrained and over-pulls past the drawer limit.
\end{itemize}
Improvements: (a) one collision-aware facade whose \emph{every} stage (IK seed, joint plan,
Cartesian pull) is world-checked; (b) a multi-view (agentview$+$wrist) \emph{fused} collision
world; (c) obstacle-aware grasp/approach selection; (d) object disturbance reported as a
first-class eval metric alongside \code{check\_success}, so ``robust'' is measured, not
assumed.

\section{Tooling we had to build}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \built{} \textbf{Headless skill runner.} Every skill ran inside the LLM
        \code{exec()} loop; there was no clean ``inject \code{functions()}, run code, report
        \code{check\_success()}'' entrypoint. We built one (\code{harness.py} /
        \code{run\_robust.py}) --- also the shape of the self-evolve Benchmark Evaluator's
        \code{eval\_fn} and of regression tests.
  \item \built{} \textbf{Multi-round sim-driving loop.} cap-x had no way to ``run a segment
        $\to$ observe sensing$+$GT $\to$ write/run the next segment'' against a live env while
        keeping the (expensive, $\sim$2\,min compile) planner warm. We built
        \code{interactive.py} $+$ \code{ictl.sh}: load env $+$ planner once, then exec Python
        snippets from a file inbox in a persistent namespace with \code{reset\_env(s)} for the
        reset-on-collision rule. Nearly every design discovery came from it.
  \item \built{} \textbf{Depth$\to$collision-world HORL planner} (\code{horl\_planner.py}):
        HORL RRT$+$trajopt wrapped for cap-x, collision world built from the agentview depth
        cloud; fixed horizon $+$ fixed sphere pool $\Rightarrow$ JAX compiles once then
        $\sim$0.6\,s/solve.
  \item \flag{} Ergonomic foot-guns: the control-API ctor eagerly warms up pyroki (needs the
        server even for skills that never use IK); the pyroki server has no \code{/health}
        route (404); raw obs depth is $(H,W,1)$ while the reduced API squeezes to $(H,W)$;
        the in-repo \code{*\_CODE} examples call functions not in the current
        \code{functions()} (example rot).
\end{itemize}

\section{What we added over stock cap-x to reach 5/5}
The win came from one decisive change plus the supports that let it land safely every time
(detail in \code{DESIGN\_SUMMARY.md}):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Deterministic IK seat} (\S\ref{sec:seat}) --- replaced the stochastic trajopt
        seat. This is what took success from 3/5 to a deterministic 5/5.
  \item \textbf{Depth-point-cloud collision world} $+$ HORL planner so transit routes
        \emph{around} the scene objects instead of through them ($\sim$210\,mm $\to$ tens of
        mm).
  \item \textbf{Sensing-based safety gate}, not the planner's internal cost: accept a seat by
        the executed TCP ($\code{on\_bar}$) and gripper reading, not by trajopt cost
        (which false-positives).
  \item \textbf{Pose-verified descend-to-pre} (RRT silent-fail fallback) and \textbf{trajopt
        retreat} (RRT-hang fix).
  \item \textbf{Geometry-aware grasp}: recognize the D-bracket bar (perpendicular to the
        $+Y$ pull), center the grip vertically on the bar, descend through the clear gap
        between the plate edge and the handle, and tune the pull to stop right at the drawer
        travel.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Prioritized recommendations}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{One consistent task/eval contract} --- single source for instruction $+$
        reward, asserted at load (\S\ref{sec:contract}).
  \item \textbf{One tested motion facade with a headless runner} --- IK, free-space plan,
        collision-aware plan, \emph{and a deterministic short-range seat}; CI smoke test that
        plans$+$executes one grasp; delete the dead paths (\S\ref{sec:seat}, \S4).
  \item \textbf{Contact/collision feedback $+$ observed-scene collision planning} (\S6),
        plus an \textbf{operational-space / compliant control mode} (\S7).
  \item \textbf{A small articulated-manipulation atomic library} ---
        \code{estimate\_front\_normal}, \code{front\_grasp}, \code{probe\_prismatic\_axis},
        \code{pull\_open} --- exactly the atoms this task needed (\S8).
  \item \textbf{Object disturbance as a first-class eval metric} alongside success (\S9).
\end{enumerate}
The perception $+$ planning \emph{ingredients} are good; they are not yet assembled into a
reliable, observable, eval-consistent control loop. With the above, both of cap-x's stated
north-stars --- benchmark accuracy and faster human-in-the-loop success --- get materially
easier.
